Independent human transcript study

Can sequence features predict RefSeq coding annotation?

A reproducible, preliminary benchmark asks whether sequence features distinguish human transcripts annotated with a coding sequence (CDS) from explicitly annotated noncoding RNAs in one pinned GRCh38 annotation snapshot.

On the grouped held-out set, the simple length, GC, and 3-mer model and the character 3–5-mer TF-IDF model reach similar AUPRC (0.7695 and 0.7699). Source and labels

The notebook used NCBI human assembly GCF_000001405.40 (GRCh38.p14), annotation release GCF_000001405.40-RS_2025_08. A coding label requires a GFF3 transcript-to-CDS relationship on one of the 25 assembled molecules (chromosomes 1–22, X, Y, and mitochondrion). A noncoding label requires no such CDS and an explicit noncoding RNA feature or biotype. NM_/NR_ prefixes are used as a consistency check, not as the label rule.

The GFF3 audit counted 4,932,571 rows: 4,487,978 on the included assembled molecules and 444,593 outside that set, which were excluded. Of 93,550 versioned FASTA accessions, 93,349 matched the annotation: 69,608 coding, 22,057 explicit noncoding, 1,684 pseudogene, and 201 unmatched. There were no ambiguous labels, prefix disagreements, conflicting FASTA duplicates, invalid sequences, transcripts shorter than 20 nt, or accessions with multiple primary GeneIDs. Pseudogenes and unresolved records are excluded from modeling.

Kaggle expanded the RNA FASTA and GFF3 before execution, so original NCBI MD5 verification was unavailable for those expanded files. Their pinned SHA-256 checks passed; the assembly report's NCBI MD5 and SHA-256 checks passed. The bundled MMseqs2 executable hash also matched.

Leakage control and evaluation

The audit covered the full parsed accession set. For modeling, the notebook sampled up to 3,000 eligible transcripts per label with seed 42. Groups joined shared GeneID, exact-sequence matches, MMseqs2 clusters, and directly detected cross-split sequence links. The first 80% identity / 80% coverage boundary search found 10 hits; nine new links were added, the split was repaired, and the next round found zero. The final train/validation/test searches, on both strands at search sensitivity 7, reported zero qualifying hits. This is evidence conditional on the stated heuristic search, not proof that no similar sequences exist.

Error review

Overall error was 20.8% among coding test transcripts and 33.1% among noncoding ones. The sampled noncoding misc_rna group had the largest reviewed error rate (191 records; 81.7%; median length 3,274 nt); the 348 ncrna records had 12.9% error, and 66 ncrna;precursor_rna records had no errors in this test sample. Those subgroup counts and rates are descriptive and may reflect this release's annotation composition.

Limits and next review

This is prediction of one annotation snapshot, not a translation or expression assay and not a functional or clinical classifier. The sample is capped at 3,000 transcripts per label; transcripts are sampled uniformly and metrics are transcript-weighted, so genes with many isoforms can contribute more. The similarity search is heuristic and depends on its settings. Independent assembly validation and external bioinformatics review are pending. Do not use the model to assert that a transcript is translated.

A useful next check is an independent chromosome or assembly holdout and expert review of the GFF3 label rules and the high-error misc_rna subgroup. FLIP2 results remain on their own track; variants are outside this study.
